\begin{abstract}
Vision--language models fail on rendered scientific figures, and the failure is widely attributed to
perception rather than reasoning. The evidence is usually a two-stage pipeline that replaces the model's
perception with a model-written description. That design compares two model configurations and cannot say
how much information the image contains. We supply the missing quantity. A crystal-structure render is a
known orthographic projection of a known structure, so the information it carries can be measured by
inverting the cameras and applying a deterministic symmetry algorithm, with no model anywhere in the
measurement. On a certified-label benchmark of crystal-system classification, this geometric oracle
recovers 0.9524 and 0.9095 of structures on two 210-structure samples, against 0.6905 for the strongest
model arm (paired per structure, discordance $61{:}6$, $p = 1.5\times10^{-12}$). Every label is generated rather than annotated,
so the ceiling and the ground truth come from one source. Using the oracle as one rung of a four-rung
attribution ladder yields two results a two-stage comparison cannot reach. First, supplying exact geometry
as text leaves 12 of the 14 scored models limited by the symbolic residual rather than by perception
(median perception share 30.9\%), and the share tracks model strength: ``perception is the bottleneck'' is
false as a general statement on this task and true only of the strongest models. Second, a strong model
used as the extraction stage emits syntactically perfect coordinate lists whose median recall against
ground truth is 0.0000, a fabrication that downstream accuracy alone would have attributed to reasoning.
The method transfers to any modality whose forward rendering is known and invertible.
\end{abstract}
