\textbf{To the Editorial Office, \\ \textit{Digital Discovery}}\\
Royal Society of Chemistry

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, ``\textbf{A model-free ceiling for vision--language model evaluation on rendered crystal
structures},'' for consideration as a Full Paper in
\textit{Digital Discovery}.

General-purpose models are increasingly asked to read rendered crystal structures, and when one answers
wrongly there is currently no way to tell whether it misread the image or misreasoned about it. Every
existing method for separating the two puts a second model in the loop, so the reference inherits that
model's errors and no quantity is ever stated for how much of the task the image answers on its own. Our
manuscript closes that gap for any benchmark built by rendering a structure whose coordinates are known:
because the cameras are fixed by the experimenter, the projection can be inverted in closed form,
cross-view correspondence re-solved, and a deterministic symmetry algorithm applied to the reconstruction,
giving the answer the images support with no model at any step.

Our main contributions are threefold. First, we prove exactly when such a ceiling is exact, through an
emptiness condition on a set of geometric phantoms, and certify by direct census that the condition holds on
all 2160 certified structures under our five-camera protocol, while failing once cameras are removed: the
ceiling is a property of the protocol, and is designable rather than given. Second, using the ceiling as the
top rung of an attribution ladder over fourteen vision--language models, we find that supplying exact
geometry as text lifts every model but closes the minority of each gap, leaving 13 of 14 limited more by
what survives perception than by perception itself; the single exception sits at the top of the roster,
which reconciles our result with prior model-in-the-loop studies that examine only frontier systems. Third,
the same instrument catches a failure no leaderboard can see: a strong model used as the structure-extraction
stage emits syntactically perfect coordinate lists whose median recall against ground truth is zero, which
downstream accuracy alone would have scored as poor reasoning.

We believe this work fits the journal's emphasis on rigorous evaluation and on reproducibility of
computational findings. The measurement is deterministic and CPU-only, every reported accuracy recomputes
from released per-structure prediction vectors without API access, and each analysis was pre-registered
before its numbers existed, including those whose outcome differed from our registered expectation. The
practical implication is direct for autonomous discovery workflows: a two-stage pipeline whose intermediate
structure cannot be checked will attribute fabrication to reasoning, and only a model-free reference
distinguishes the two.
