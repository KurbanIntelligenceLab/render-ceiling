\label{app:glance}

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proof}
Contribution (iii): the proofs of Theorem~1, Proposition~2 and Corollary~\ref{cor:design} of the article, with the mechanical checks that accompany them.


\begin{corollary}[Two design consequences]
\label{cor:design}
(a) If $\mathcal{C}$ contains two independent directions then, at $\tau = 0$,
$\Phi_0(s, \mathcal{C} \cup \{c\}) \subseteq \Phi_0(s, \mathcal{C})$: adding a view can never destroy
identifiability. (b) If $d_v \parallel t$ for a lattice vector $t \in L$ then $\Pi_v(p + kt) = \Pi_v(p)$, so
view $v$ separates no atoms differing by multiples of $t$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Phantom set, componentwise]
\label{def:phantom-full}
Fix $\tau \ge 0$. A species-labelled point $(p, z)$ lies in $\Phi_\tau(s,\mathcal{C})$ if
(i) $\|p - L x_i\| > \tau$ for every atom $i$ with $z_i = z$;
(ii) there are views $v \neq w$ and atoms $a \neq b$ of species $z$ with $\Pi_v(p) = \Pi_v(L x_a)$ and
$\Pi_w(p) = \Pi_w(L x_b)$; and
(iii) every view $u \in \mathcal{C}$ has an atom $i$ of species $z$ with
$\|\Pi_u(p) - \Pi_u(L x_i)\| \le \tau$.
The set depends on $s$ and $\mathcal{C}$ alone.
\end{definition}

\textbf{Why the tolerances must be tied.} The merge in (c) is performed at $\delta$ while the label map
decides at $\tau$. If $\delta > \tau$, two genuinely distinct atoms separated by more than $2\tau$ can still
fall within $\delta$ of one another and be merged; the surviving point is displaced by up to $\delta/2$, which
exceeds the resolution at which $\spg$ distinguishes configurations, so the reconstruction can carry the
correct atom count and still return the wrong symmetry. Keeping $\delta \le \tau$ removes that mode and makes
the merge the one (c) assumes.

\textbf{Theorem~1(a) of the article.} Let atom $a$ sit at $p$ and let $v,w$ have linearly independent
directions. Its images are $q = P_v p$ and $q' = P_w p$, and the back-projections are the affine lines
$\ell_v = \{x : P_v x = q\}$ with direction $d_v$ and $\ell_w = \{x : P_w x = q'\}$ with direction $d_w$.
Both contain $p$, and two non-parallel lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$ that meet do so in one point, so
$\ell_v \cap \ell_w = \{p\}$ and the intersection step returns $p$. Condition~(iii) holds at $p$ with
$i = a$ in every view, so $p$ is accepted. The pair was arbitrary among independent pairs, which gives
order-independence: every such pair produces the same point and the enumeration order cannot change the
accepted set.

\textbf{(b).} A point $\hat p$ accepted by Algorithm~\ref{alg:oracle} arises as the intersection of the
back-projections of two same-species atom images, which is Definition~\ref{def:phantom-full}(ii), and passes
verification in the remaining views; since it also projects exactly onto an atom image in $v$ and in $w$, it
satisfies~(iii) in every view. If in addition $\hat p$ is further than $\tau$ from all same-species atoms
then~(i) holds and $\hat p \in \Phi_\tau$. Conversely, if $(p,z) \in \Phi_\tau$ then~(ii) supplies a pair
of atom images whose back-projections the algorithm enumerates and whose intersection is $p$, and~(iii)
supplies the verification, so $p$ is accepted.

\textbf{(c).} Suppose $\Phi_\tau = \emptyset$. By (b) every accepted point lies within $\tau$ of some
same-species atom, and by (a) every atom is accepted. Merging accepted points at tolerance $\tau$ therefore
produces one cluster per atom, provided no cluster can absorb two atoms; that is exactly the separation
hypothesis, since two atoms more than $2\tau$ apart cannot both lie within $\tau$ of a common point.
Applying $\spg$ to $X$ returns $y(s)$ by the definition of the label. \hfill$\square$

\textbf{Proposition~2 of the article.} At $\varepsilon = 0$ the extractor returns the true centroids, every
quantity the pipeline compares equals the oracle's, the accepted sets coincide, and the labels agree. For
$\varepsilon > 0$, write $M = [P_v; P_w]$ for an enumerated pair. The triangulated point solves
$M \hat p = b$ with $b$ the stacked 2D observations, so perturbing $b$ by at most $2\varepsilon$ in norm
perturbs $\hat p$ by at most $\|M^{+}\|_2 \cdot 2\varepsilon \le 2\kappa\varepsilon$. Each compared
distance is between two such quantities, so it differs from the oracle's by at most $2\kappa\varepsilon$,
and a comparison against $\tau$ has the same outcome in both whenever the oracle's distance is further than
$2\kappa\varepsilon$ from $\tau$. On structures satisfying that margin the accepted sets, and hence the
labels, coincide. \hfill$\square$

The band must be taken as $2\kappa\varepsilon$ rather than $2\varepsilon$ whenever $\kappa > 1$: for two view
directions $5^\circ$ apart, $\kappa \approx 16$, so using the unscaled $2\varepsilon$ would understate the
true error band by more than an order of magnitude.

\textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:design}(a).} Let $\tau = 0$ and $(p,z) \in \Phi_0(s,\mathcal{C}\cup\{c\})$. By
(iii) every view $u \in \mathcal{C}\cup\{c\}$, in particular every $u \in \mathcal{C}$, has an atom $i_u$ of
species $z$ with $\Pi_u(p) = \Pi_u(L x_{i_u})$. Pick two independent views $v',w' \in \mathcal{C}$. If
$i_{v'} = i_{w'} = i$ then $p$ and $L x_i$ share both projections, and by the uniqueness argument of (a)
$p = L x_i$, contradicting~(i) at $\tau = 0$; so $i_{v'} \neq i_{w'}$ and~(ii) holds within $\mathcal{C}$.
Condition~(iii) holds on the smaller set because it held on the larger, and~(i) is unchanged. Hence
$(p,z) \in \Phi_0(s,\mathcal{C})$. \hfill$\square$

\textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:design}(b).} $P_v$ has rows orthonormal and orthogonal to $d_v$, so $P_v t = 0$
whenever $t \parallel d_v$, and $\Pi_v(p + kt) = P_v p + k P_v t = \Pi_v(p)$. \hfill$\square$

Every step above was checked mechanically: the two algebraic identities in a computer algebra system, the
set inclusion of Corollary~\ref{cor:design}(a) by exhaustive enumeration on random structures, and the
constant $\kappa$ and its empirical amplification for the frozen camera set numerically. The script ships
with the artifact.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Geometric oracle $O(s, \mathcal{C})$}
\label{alg:oracle}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\For{each view $v$}
  \State $Q_v \gets \{(\Pi_v(L x_i),\, z_i) : i = 1,\dots,N\}$
  \Comment{projected, species-labelled 2D points; cross-view identity discarded}
\EndFor
\For{each pair of views $(v, w)$ and each same-species pair $(q, q') \in Q_v \times Q_w$}
  \State $\hat{p} \gets$ intersection of the back-projected rays $\Pi_v^{-1}(q)$, $\Pi_w^{-1}(q')$, if they meet
  \State accept $\hat{p}$ iff its projection lies within tolerance of a same-species point in every
         remaining view
\EndFor
\State merge accepted points lying within $\delta$ of one another
\State \Return $\spg(\text{merged reconstruction})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Statistical procedures}
\label{app:stats}
The tests behind the 13-of-14 attribution finding and the fabrication scoring are specified here in full,
along with every other procedure used in Sections~3.2--3.6 of the article.

\textbf{Paired comparisons.} Every model-versus-model and model-versus-oracle comparison on a shared sample
is a paired exact binomial (sign) test on discordant structures: with $g$ structures one arm alone gets
right and $l$ the other alone, the two-sided $p$ is that of $\mathrm{Bin}(g+l, 1/2)$ evaluated at
$\min(g,l)$. For the render-convention probe, the per-comparison minimum detectable difference is the
smallest $|g-l|/n$ reaching $p<0.05$ given that comparison's observed $g+l$; with $g+l \le 5$ no split
reaches it, which is the source of the 7-of-16 unreachable comparisons in
Section~3.6 of the article.
\textbf{Perception share.} The median share is reported with a percentile bootstrap over models
($B = 20{,}000$, resampling the 14 model-level shares). Whether the post-perception residual exceeds the
perception component is a per-model binary, so the count of 13 of 14 is tested with an exact sign test
against parity. Any statement about how the share is \emph{ordered} across models is a Spearman
correlation on $n = 14$ and has no useful power; we report the correlation and decline the ordering claim.
Note that the share $P/(R_1 - R_4)$ carries $R_4$ in both numerator and denominator, so a correlation
between share and $R_4$ is partly structural and should not be read as a mechanism.

\textbf{Clustered observations.} Per-structure regressions pooled across models place 14 non-independent
observations on each structure. We report the pooled statistics as descriptive only and base every claim on
the model-level sign test, which respects the clustering.

\textbf{Atom-count association behind the residual bound (Section~3.5 of the article).} Pooling the 2940
model-structure pairs of the atom-count regression gives $\rho=-0.0908$ ($p=8.1\times10^{-7}$) and a drop
from 0.5551 to 0.5129 across the median cell size (Fisher $p=0.0241$), both descriptive only given the
clustering above. Atom count correlates with crystal system ($\rho=-0.1866$), but the mean within-system
association ($-0.1053$) is stronger than the pooled figure, so the atom-count effect behind the residual
bound is not symmetry difficulty in disguise.

\textbf{Label certification.} Label agreement is measured on a stratified sample of $n=224$ structures (112
Materials Project, 112 JARVIS-DFT, 32 per crystal system), disjoint from training and evaluation; the
stability policy quarantines 4, leaving 220 kept. The one-sided exact 95\% lower bound for $k/k$ agreements is
$0.05^{1/k}$; for $k = 220$ this is $0.9865$. The source databases reach spglib through pymatgen and JARVIS's
own wrapper respectively.
\textbf{Run-to-run spread.} The second sweep execution is retained as a labelled replication; spread is
reported as the mean and maximum absolute per-model difference in correct counts.

\section{Label granularity}
\label{app:granularity}
This section supports the granularity claim of Section~2.3 of the article: the identifiability result is a
property of the reconstruction and not of the seven-way label. We score four labels of increasing granularity
for the model-free arms. The oracle is flat across granularity because a
reconstruction is either right or wrong and all four labels follow from it; the cell-metric baseline falls
from 0.8952 at crystal system to 0.6810 at space group, because point group and space group depend on atom
positions that lattice parameters do not encode. The identifiability claim is therefore not specific to the
seven-way label.
\section{Tabular classifier: feature specification and reading sensitivity}
\label{app:classifier}
The cell-metric random forest of Section~3.2 of the article (0.8952 on crystal system) uses 19 features: the
six lattice parameters and cell volume, three scale-free edge-length ratios, four angle-deviation terms, an
angle range, an angle-dispersion term, an edge-dispersion term, site count and density. The scale-free
ratios are defined as ratios of \emph{sorted} edge lengths (min/mid, mid/max, min/max) rather than of
edges in their native axis order, and the dispersion terms are population standard deviations; both choices
are made for invariance to axis labelling, a property a scale-free descriptor must have. Under this
canonical specification the classifier reaches $188/210=0.8952$.
Twelve combinatorially possible readings of the same specification (crossing edge-ratio sortedness with
the dispersion convention) span exactly four distinct values: 183, 184, 187 and 188 of 210. The canonical
form is the top-scoring reading, and its selection is justified by the invariance argument on its own
merits rather than by where it ranks. We report the achievable range rather than a single number because a
reader recomputing the specification from its natural-language description could reach any of the four
values depending on which convention they infer for edge-ratio ordering and dispersion.

\textbf{Noise-tolerance crossings.} The frozen protocol holds $\Rone \ge 0.95$ to $\sigma = 0.0146$\,\AA{}
against $0.0069$\,\AA{} for the off-axis set and $0.0048$\,\AA{} for the tiled arm, so tripling $\kappa$ costs
roughly half the noise tolerance, in the direction Proposition~2 of the article requires. Paired
per-structure comparison of frozen against off-axis gives no discordance at $\sigma \le 0.003$\,\AA{}, then 18
structures against 3 at $\sigma = 0.01$\,\AA{} ($p = 0.0015$). At $\delta \le \tau$ the five-camera set reaches
1.0000 and structure-level violations of monotonicity fall to 9 of 11{,}550 nested comparisons, against 195 of
22{,}050 at the released tolerance. An off-axis set does not raise the ceiling, as reading the same
measurement at $\delta > \tau$ would suggest; that apparent gain disappears once the tolerances are tied.

\textbf{Scope guidance from Proposition~2 of the article.} The constant rises steeply as two view directions
approach parallel: $\kappa = 16.2108$ at $5^\circ$ separation and $81.0295$ at $1^\circ$. Within the pipeline
class the proposition covers, the render choices that keep an extraction stage inside it are those that keep
centroids well defined: flat shading, atom radii small enough that same-species discs do not merge in
projection, and bond geometry that does not bias a disc's centre. The proposition also states what a
sufficient extraction experiment must show, namely bounded centroid error with no spurious detections rather
than high recall alone.

\textbf{Why the two predictions failed.} The three-way ordering treated tiling as $\kappa$-preserving, which
it is not: tiling multiplies atoms per cell eightfold and shrinks same-species separations, so it degrades
robustness through density rather than through camera geometry, and that arm therefore cannot score $\kappa$
in either direction. The absolute budget failed because the band of Proposition~2 of the article is
sufficient rather than tight: the binding constraint is the displacement at which $\spg$ flips, not the
distance at which two atoms collide, which is why the predicted $\sigma = 0.4456$\,\AA{} overstates the
measured crossing at $0.0146$\,\AA{} by a factor of 31.

\textbf{Per-pair conditioning constants.} The constant of Proposition~2 of the article, evaluated over the ten
independent pairs of the five-camera set. Frozen: 1.0000 (axis-$a$/axis-$b$), 1.0000 ($a$/$c$), 1.4142
($a$/body-diagonal), 2.7321 ($a$/oblique-2), 1.0000 ($b$/$c$), 2.0000 ($b$/body-diagonal), 1.1547
($b$/oblique-2), 1.3280 ($c$/body-diagonal), 1.3280 ($c$/oblique-2), 2.2823 (body-diagonal/oblique-2); the
maximum is 2.7321. Off-axis: the maximum rises to 8.4258, on the same $a$/oblique-2 pair. A random-perturbation
check reproduces the frozen maximum empirically at 2.7318 over $2\times10^{4}$ draws restricted to realizable
perturbations. This value supersedes an earlier one of 1.7689 for the same protocol, which equals $1.3280^2$
for the constant of two other pairs and so appears to arise from an algebraic error in squaring; no
formulation we tried (stacked, transposed, horizontally stacked, Frobenius, or the ratio of extreme singular
values) reproduces it. The band half-width $2\kappa\varepsilon$ at the measured median extraction error
therefore moves from $0.0902$\,\AA{} to $0.1393$\,\AA{}, and the conclusion it supports is unchanged: the
closest compared distance in the sample, $1.0951$\,\AA{}, still clears the band's upper edge by a factor of
7.34.

\textbf{Phantom density.} The dependence of $|\Phi_0|$ on atoms per cell does not reduce to one coefficient,
and the pooled Spearman correlation changes sign with view count: $+0.4571$ at two views, $-0.1471$ at three
($n = 1950$ structures, ten subsets each; this census set is the one described in
Section~3.4 of the article). Decomposing resolves it. Conditional on $\Phi_0$ being nonempty the
count climbs steeply with density at both view counts ($\rho = 0.8331$, $n = 1408$ at two views;
$\rho = 0.8273$, $n = 365$ at three). What differs is the probability of being nonempty at all: flat in density
at two views ($\rho = 0.0299$, $p = 0.19$) and falling at three ($\rho = -0.1875$, $p = 6.9\times10^{-17}$).
The three-view negative is therefore a mixture effect, larger cells being less likely to admit any phantom
under three-view verification while producing many when they do, and is not evidence that density protects
against phantoms. This is the measurement Section~2.1 of the article promises in place of a proved rate; we
report the decomposition and not the pooled coefficient.

\textbf{Tiling and identifiability.} The released tiling's translations parallel three of the five cameras,
so by Corollary~\ref{cor:design}(b) those three separate no atoms differing by them; the two oblique cameras
do, and clause (a) then leaves identifiability intact, so at exact extraction the tiled geometry returns every
label. What tiling costs is conditioning rather than identifiability, since it multiplies atoms per cell
eightfold and shrinks same-species separations, which is the arm measured in Table~\ref{tab:conventions}.

\textbf{Why the tolerance frontier of Table~\ref{tab:frontier} is not perfectly monotone at the tight end.}
The extraction-tolerance frontier reported in Section~3.4 of the article has one non-monotone point: at
$n=1933$, $\delta = 0.005$\,\AA{} costs one structure where $\delta = 0.01$\,\AA{} costs none. This is the
opposite failure mode from the over-merging that costs the frontier elsewhere: too tight a $\delta$ can fail
to combine two ray-intersection candidates that triangulate the same atom from different view pairs, so the
reconstruction reports two points where one atom exists, rather than one point where two atoms exist. Both
failure directions are consistent with the merge step of Algorithm~\ref{alg:oracle} and with
Theorem~1(c) of the article, which assumes $\delta \le \tau$ rather than $\delta \to 0$.

\textbf{Power against the models.} Four strong models across five conventions on a stratified 70-structure
subsample give zero significant changes in 16 paired comparisons, with near-symmetric discordance. Power
is low and we state it per comparison: discordance averages 6.3 of 70, and significance is reachable in
only 9 of the 16, the other 7 having too few discordant pairs for any split to reach $p<0.05$
(Section~\ref{app:stats} gives the procedure and the per-comparison minimum detectable differences, which
run 0.0857 to 0.1286 across the 9 comparisons where significance is reachable at all). The largest effect
observed anywhere in the probe is 0.0857, and it occurred in a comparison whose own threshold was higher
than that, so no comparison came within its own detection limit. These are paired per-comparison
thresholds; a single pooled minimum detectable difference computed under an unpaired approximation is not
valid for this design and is not reported, and the per-comparison analysis it would have replaced makes the
null weaker rather than stronger. We therefore do not claim the null bounds
the effect at any particular size; what it supports is that no convention produced a detectable change,
and that for seven comparisons the design could not have detected one.

\section{Secondary axes and supporting figures}
\label{app:secondary}
No main-text claim rests on this section; the leaderboard and the per-model image-removal control are in the
main text (Section~3.1 of the article, Section~3.5 of the article), and this section carries the remaining
secondary-axis figures.

\textbf{Cue sufficiency.} Whether the conventional-cell metric alone determines the crystal system splits
the samples $140/70$ and $141/69$ and is a stable property of the render convention with no accuracy
mechanism attached, testing four independent ways for pixel accuracy to simply drop on the ambiguous
stratum and finding none that holds. A contrast against a cell-metric control does widen on that stratum for
all three frontier models on both samples (Figure~\ref{fig:cuesuff}), but the stratum is $60/70$ and
$58/69$ hexagonal-or-trigonal, so the finding is close to a restatement of that one degeneracy: the
$69$-structure sample's residual after removing that degeneracy is $69-58=11$ structures (5 orthorhombic,
5 tetragonal, 1 cubic), and the $70$-structure sample's is $n=10$.

\textbf{One generation of model progress.} It does not erase the axis (Figure~\ref{fig:generational}): from
\rlOldGen{} to \rlLadder{} (identifiers in Section~3.1 of the article), which is the same arm that heads the
ladder, the newer model gains more on the ambiguous stratum in raw terms but

model pair is a comparison rather than a trend.

\section{Extended limitations}
\label{app:extraction}
Each limitation Section~3.7 of the article states in brief is given in full here.

\textbf{Extraction share.} $R_1$ assumes ideal extraction, so it cannot by itself say how much of the
ceiling-to-model gap a real extraction stage would close. The one extractor evaluated end to end at the
pixel level, a connected-component blob detector working from colour thresholds, was scored against its own
pre-registered 5\% triangulation-failure gate and failed it on two independently drawn samples (40/210 =
19.0\% and 47/210 = 22.4\% of structures recovering zero atoms), so it is reported as a diagnostic rather than
entered into the ladder. Its two recall figures measure different things and should not be compared directly:
0.400 is the median per-structure recall of reconstructed atoms reported in Section~3.5 of the article, while
0.4227 below is the median per-view recall of 2D detections.

That failure is not an anomaly but what Proposition~2 of the article predicts, and the margins are
measurable. Characterising the same detector against the proposition's own hypothesis over all 210
evaluation structures and all five frozen views (1{,}050 view measurements, 54{,}258 matched detections)
gives median precision 0.4278 and median recall 0.4227, so it is neither sound nor complete: most
detections are spurious and roughly 58\% of atoms are missed outright at any tolerance. Localisation fails
by a wide margin even on the atoms it does find and match, with median centroid error $0.0329$~\AA{}
against the $\tau/2 = 0.005$~\AA{} the proposition requires, a factor of $6.6$; only 38 of 54{,}258 matched
centroids ($0.07\%$) meet the requirement. Taking $\varepsilon$ per structure as the median centroid error,
the ambiguity band $[\tau - 2\kappa\varepsilon,\ \tau + 2\kappa\varepsilon]$ has median half-width
$0.1393$~\AA{} and every structure's compared distances avoid it, but that check is uninformative here: the
closest interatomic distance anywhere in the sample is $1.0951$~\AA{}, about seven times the band's upper
edge, so no physically realisable structure could fail it at this tolerance.

The limitation is therefore that the available detector is too weak to attribute the gap, not that the
measurement is missing. Closing it needs an extractor roughly seven times better at localisation and
materially better at both soundness and completeness, which is a new method rather than a rerun; such an
extractor, meeting the condition of Proposition~2 of the article, is the single most valuable missing
measurement.

\textbf{Fabrication denominator.} In the learned-extractor fabrication finding of
Section~3.5 of the article, the denominator is 206 rather than the full $n=210$: four
structures (\texttt{mp-1391233},
\texttt{mp-733975}, \texttt{mp-1104047}, \texttt{mp-1214794}) elicited no parseable atom list and fail the
pre-registered unparseable- and fewer-than-3-atoms gates, so they are excluded from the median-recall
denominator while counting as unanswered in the full-$n{=}210$ accuracy figure. This is the intermediate a
model-in-the-loop split has no model-free reference to check, which is why the fabrication would have read
as bad reasoning under such a split rather than as fabrication.

\textbf{Roster currency and decode protocol.} The rosters were frozen before the current frontier
generation and carry no arm beyond the three frontier models already compared in
Section~\ref{app:secondary}, so the leaderboard is a snapshot rather than a ranking; the gap could narrow
under a stronger roster. No prompt-sensitivity sweep was run: each condition used one verbatim prompt with no
per-model tuning, and the $R_3$ condition supplied geometry in a single normalised textual schema, so
differences between plausible schemas (for instance a raw crystallographic file against normalised fields)
are untested.

The scored arms set no reasoning or extended-thinking parameter, so each ran at its provider's platform
default rather than at a controlled budget. One model was tested against a controlled budget, and on that
model the setting does not move accuracy; the axis is uncontrolled for the remaining arms, so what follows
bounds the effect for a single frontier model rather than for the roster. Running \rlBudget{} (identifier
in Section~3.1 of the article) on
the full original sample at $K{=}3$ under two settings,
a minimal reasoning budget and the provider default, gives \emph{identical} accuracy, $130/210 = 0.6190$
under both, while the default emits $5.4\times$ the output tokens (482 against 89 per call) and takes
$3.4\times$ the wall time. The totals are not the same predictions: the settings agree on $169/210$ and
break even on the discordant ones, 13 against 13, an exact two-sided $p = 1.0000$. The default also
returned empty content on 11 of 210 structures where the minimal budget returned none, and restricting to
the 199 both settings answered leaves them indistinguishable ($0.6332$ against $0.6533$, $p = 0.5235$).
The reshuffling is structured even though the total does not move: extra deliberation helps on tetragonal
and trigonal and hurts on hexagonal and triclinic, the strata where the cell metric is closest to
degenerate, though at $n=30$ per stratum those differences are individually underpowered and we report
them as description. The relevant consequence for this paper is that for this model the distance to the
ceiling is not a deliberation deficit: $5.4\times$ more reasoning closes none of it. Whether that holds
across the roster is untested, since only this one arm was run at a controlled budget.

\textbf{Sample size and feature-definition sensitivity.} All model numbers rest on two samples of 210,
where the minimum detectable paired difference is near 0.09, so adjacent leaderboard rows are not
separable. The model arms were not re-run at $n=1933$, so the gap at scale is bounded on the oracle side
only; the oracle and both classical baselines are complete on the full 1933. The tabular classifier of
Section~3.2 of the article reaches 183 to 188 of 210 across defensible readings of its feature
specification, with the canonical form at the top of that range (Section~\ref{app:classifier}); the
cue-sufficiency stratum of Section~\ref{app:secondary} rests on similarly small strata ($n=10$ and
$n=11$) once its one dominant degeneracy is removed.

\textbf{Size-matched control for the scale-up baseline (Section~3.5 of the article).} The $n=1933$ sample has
larger cells than the original (median 38 atoms against 14), so the drop in the shape-free baseline at scale
(0.5286 to 0.2054) could in principle be a cell-size artefact rather than a property of the larger draw. A
control restricted to the $n=1933$ structures at or below the original sample's 95th-percentile cell size
puts the baseline at 0.2205, close to the unrestricted 0.2054 and far from the original 0.5286, so the shift
survives size-matching and is a property of the draw.

\textbf{Beyond orthographic cameras: full statement.} The identifiability argument does not depend on orthography as heavily
as the algebra suggests, and the changes a calibrated perspective protocol would require are worth stating
even though we have not measured them. Back-projections become rays through a known camera centre rather than
parallel lines, so two rays from independent views still meet in one point and Theorem~1(a) of the article
survives with the intersection argument unchanged; the phantom-set characterisation in (b) is likewise a
statement about coincident projections and does not use parallelism. Two things do change.
Corollary~\ref{cor:design}(b) fails, since a perspective camera does not project lattice-translated copies
onto the same point, which removes the tiling degeneracy but also removes the clean statement about it. And
$\kappa$ would have to be redefined from the projective Jacobian at each point rather than from a fixed
$2\times3$ matrix, so conditioning becomes position-dependent and the single per-protocol constant of
Proposition~2 of the article becomes a field over the scene. Renderers that are not injective on the visible
content, such as those producing identical silhouettes for distinct objects, fall outside the construction
altogether rather than merely complicating it.

\textbf{Superseded reading of the off-axis protocol.} Read at $\delta > \tau$, the off-axis camera set
appears to raise the ceiling above the frozen protocol. That ordering is an artefact of merging above the
symmetry tolerance rather than a property of the cameras, and it disappears once $\delta \le \tau$, at which
point the two protocols are equally exact and separate only under centroid noise
(Section~3.6 of the article). The superseded reading is released with the surviving one.

\textbf{Values that do not reproduce from their own records.} Three quantities in the project's history
could not be recovered from the specifications that produced them, and are reported as unrecoverable
rather than replaced by a nearby figure: a cue-sufficiency stratification rule, whose ambiguous-stratum
count is reproducible but whose stratum composition is not, under a fifty-combination grid over
tolerances and branch orderings; an oracle view-count curve, which was computed against a live database
query whose candidate pool drifts between runs at a fixed seed and so cannot be re-derived on the same
structures; and two earlier readings of the tabular classifier, one whose feature list was never saved
and one that no defensible reading of the saved specification reaches. The superseded values are
released as data alongside the surviving ones so a reader can see exactly what moved.

\section{Reproducibility record}
\label{app:repro}
The released artifact contains the render protocol, the labelling tolerances and sweep, the exact feature specification
and seed for the cell-metric reference, the verbatim model prompts, and per-structure predictions for every
arm reported here, so each accuracy in this paper recomputes from released vectors without API access.
Every numeric claim was generated from the corresponding released numerical record rather than
transcribed, and a verification step refuses any manuscript sentence carrying a value absent from those
records or an accuracy without its sample and decode budget. Each analysis was pre-registered before its
numbers existed; the pre-registrations are released alongside the results, including for the analyses whose
outcome differed from the registered expectation. Dataset metadata is provided in Croissant format
validated against the reference library.

\textbf{Provenance and access.} The structures are drawn from a public crystallographic database under its
stated licence, and every render, label and prediction vector is released with the paper. The models
evaluated are third-party systems accessed through their public interfaces under their terms of service;
their identifiers and decode budgets are reported in the roster of Section~3.1 of the article rather than
anonymised, since the accuracy claims are only interpretable against a named arm, and the main text refers
to each by a role label defined from that same roster. The work involves no human subjects and no personally
identifying data; recruitment of qualified crystallographers for a human baseline was attempted and failed,
so no human-subjects protocol was executed and no human-performance claim is made.

\textbf{Compute.} The oracle and label pipeline are deterministic and CPU-only, 19--99 s per evaluation
pass; the exact processor is not recorded. Fine-tuning used a single rented consumer GPU; the headline
fine-tuned arm trained for 8 hours 12 minutes. Model arms ran through a commercial inference aggregator;
per-condition call counts range from 3{,}780 to 28{,}350, and no aggregate spend was recorded. For
locally-run decoding, sampling used temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.95, majority vote over $K$ samples, and a
900-token budget; per-model decode settings and the verbatim prompts are in the released prompt
specification. No reasoning
or extended-thinking parameter was set in any scored request, so every model ran at its provider's own
default; the one condition that varies the budget deliberately is the paired contrast described above, whose
two legs are released with the per-structure predictions.
